- data and AI are connected with improved access to health services and preventive care;
- the strategy covers skills, policy and regulation, investment, research and innovation, and ecosystem development;
- public targets and component descriptions are available through the strategy and Saudipedia overview.

Source hierarchy:
The SDAIA page and PDF are primary. Saudipedia is public background. Regulations.AI is a secondary plain-language index, not an official source or legal advice.

Application relevance:
The workspace maps research evidence, governance, human oversight, and future local validation to the national strategy. The current ADNI evidence is not a Saudi-population validation study.

PDPL and AI-ethics cross-check:
- PDPL Implementing Regulations Article 25 identifies circumstances requiring a written impact assessment, including sensitive data, linked datasets, new technologies or fully automated decisions, and processing likely to cause serious harm.
- Article 26 requires organizational, administrative, and technical safeguards for health data and measures preventing incompatible use.
- Article 33 requires records of processing activities.
- A lawful basis must be documented. Consent applies where it is the relevant basis; it is not described as the only possible basis.
- The SDAIA AI Ethics Self-Assessment supports structured ethical review but is not, by itself, legal, ethical, clinical, or regulatory certification.

Governance boundary:
Use de-identified, purpose-limited data; retain access and processing records; require clinician confirmation; and prohibit unconfirmed AI flags from advertising, unrelated profiling, insurance eligibility, autonomous diagnosis, or treatment decisions. Complete institutional privacy, legal, security, ethics, and clinical-governance review before any pilot.
